## Checks before anyone works with children

Providers must make sure the people looking after children are **suitable**. They need the right qualifications and training, and must have passed any required checks.

- Every person aged 16 and over who works directly with children, or lives or works on the premises, needs an **enhanced criminal records check** and a **barred list check**. This includes volunteers, with a few limited exceptions.
- Anyone who has lived or worked abroad should have an additional criminal records check.
- Nobody should start work until the provider has received their check.
- Someone whose suitability has not been checked must **never** have unsupervised contact with children.

---

## References, records and telling us

- Get at least one **written reference** before someone is recruited. Do not accept open references such as "to whom it may concern", and do not rely on the applicant to get it.
- The provider records qualifications, identity checks, vetting and references, including the check reference number, the date and who obtained it.
- Staff must tell the provider about anything that may affect their suitability to work with children, before or during their employment.
- If a member of staff is dismissed because they have harmed a child or put a child at risk, the provider must refer them to the Disclosure and Barring Service.

---

Key points:
- No check received, no starting work.
- Unchecked means never alone with children.
- Tell your manager about anything that may affect your suitability.
