## Noticing

Concerns can come from what a child says, how they behave, how they look, or what happens around them. Be alert to changes and patterns, not just single moments. You do not need to be sure that something is wrong before you speak up.

---

## What to do

If you are worried about a child:

- tell your DSL straight away, following your setting's procedure
- write down what you saw or heard, with the date and time, in plain facts
- keep what you know private, and share it only with the people who need to know

---

Key points:
- If a provider has concerns about a child's safety or welfare, it must immediately tell its local authority children's social care team, in line with local reporting procedures.
- In an emergency, the police are told too.
- Your DSL usually makes that contact, so tell them without delay.

---

## Absences

Providers must follow up on absences in a timely manner. If a child is away for a long time, or away without a message from their parent or carer, the setting must try to contact the parents or carers and the emergency contacts. Any concerns go to children's social care, and a police welfare check can be requested.

---

> Warning: never promise a child, or anyone else, that you will keep a worry secret. Always pass it to your DSL.
