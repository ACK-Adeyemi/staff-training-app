## Allegations against staff

Your setting's safeguarding policy must say what happens if someone makes an allegation against a member of staff. If you hear one, tell your DSL or manager straight away and write down what you were told. Do not investigate it yourself, and do not discuss it with other colleagues.

---

## Telling Ofsted

A registered provider must tell Ofsted about any allegations of harm or abuse by anyone living, working or looking after children at the premises. This applies whether the allegation is about something on the premises or elsewhere, for example on a visit.

Ofsted must be told as soon as is reasonably practicable, and in any event within **14 days** of the allegation being made. The provider must also say what action it has taken.

---

## Whistleblowing

Every setting must have a whistleblowing procedure for all staff, including students and volunteers. It must explain when and how to report concerns about poor or unsafe practice, and what happens next. Your concerns must be taken seriously.

---

Key points:
- Raise concerns about poor or unsafe practice using your setting's whistleblowing procedure.
- If you feel unable to raise something with your employer, or your genuine concerns are not being addressed, you can use other routes.
- The NSPCC whistleblowing advice line is one of them. Ofsted also has a way to make a complaint about a provider.

---

> You will not be in trouble for raising a genuine concern. Speaking up is part of keeping children safe.
