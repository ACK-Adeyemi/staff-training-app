## Getting the children out

When the alarm sounds, the nearest adult takes charge of the children in front of them. Stay calm and speak in a clear, steady voice.

- Stop the activity. Tell the children what is happening in simple words, such as "We are going outside now."
- Take the **daily register** or the room list if it is within reach and safe to do so. Never go back for it.
- Help the children line up at the door, or gather them in a group if they are very young.
- Use the **nearest safe exit**. If that exit is blocked or has smoke or flames beyond it, use the next one.
- Close doors behind you as you go if it is safe to do so. A closed door slows the spread of fire and smoke.

---

## Babies in cots

Babies who cannot walk need an adult to carry them or move them.

- Practitioners with babies carry the youngest first, one baby in each arm if you have been shown how.
- Use the **evacuation cots** (the wheeled cots with sturdy handles) in the baby room. Place up to four babies in one cot and wheel it to the nearest exit. At Little Acorns the baby room evacuation cots are kept next to the baby room door.
- Do not wait to wrap babies up. Take a blanket from the cot only if it is within reach.
- If a baby is asleep in a cot, lift the baby out. Do not wheel a cot with a sleeping baby in it down steps.

---

## Toddlers and children who need extra help

- Hold the hand of children who are unsteady. Take the toddlers' hands in a line, or use the walking rope if your room has one.
- A child with a disability, a medical need or a worry about noise has a **personal emergency plan**. Check where it is kept and who helps them out.
- A child who is scared or refuses to move: stay with them, speak gently, and pick them up if you need to. Do not leave anyone behind.

---

## Checking rooms on the way out

- Check toilets, cloakrooms, the sleep room and any quiet corners on your way out. Look quickly and do not search for long.
- Put a **red card** on the door of any room you have checked, so that nobody checks it twice.
- Never use the lift. Use the stairs or the ground-floor exit.

---

## Practice makes it calm

Fire drills are held often enough that children and staff know what to do. At Little Acorns there is a drill at least once every term, and a drill at a different time of day for new starters.

- Take part in every drill as if it were real.
- Tell the person in charge afterwards if anything got in the way, such as a cot that was hard to move or a door that stuck.
- If you start in the middle of a term, ask your Room Leader to walk you through the exits for your room in your first week.

---

Key points:
- Use the nearest safe exit. Never go back for belongings.
- Carry or wheel babies out. Use the evacuation cots in the baby room.
- Check each room on your way out and put a red card on the door.
- Take the register if it is safe. Never go back for it.
